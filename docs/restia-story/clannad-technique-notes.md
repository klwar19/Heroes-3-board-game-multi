# Clannad technique notes (for the Restia / Bin prologue writers)

Source: the English CLANNAD script transcripts (`G:\games\CLANNAD\txt\seenNNNN.txt`). Sampled:
opening days 0414-0418, the doorway skits (0424), the baseball game (0429), the Tomoyo kick scenes
(2416, 2418), the Nagisa route (6501), After Story (6800, 7200), the illusory-world interludes
(6900), the Fuko ending (1517-1518) and the Kotomi flashback (4512). Everything below is
paraphrased. Short quoted fragments are marked and kept to a minimum.

Note on the transcripts: they are flattened, so both branches of a choice appear one after the other,
and some italic words were dropped (blank gaps like "The running of the / ."). Read around them.

## 1. Techniques

### T1. Short narration, quick hand-offs
**Clannad:** On comedy days narration runs about 2 lines between spoken lines (0415-0417, 6501:
average run 1.8-2.0). A narration line is about 9-10 words. Long narration only shows up for
backstory told on a quiet day (0414, the father monologue: about 50 lines in a row) or for a
flashback (4512, Kotomi's garden). Then it snaps back to dialogue.
**Ours right now:** narrator lines average 14-16 words and runs of 4-7 lines (p5Eos 7.0,
p0Saturdays 6.6). p3TrashCan has a 27-line narration run during the chase.
**How we use it:** In comedy, no more than 3 narrator lines in a row before someone speaks, reacts
or makes a sound. Save the long 45-word lines for scene openings and the one quiet line. At a
climax, keep narrator lines to 12 words or fewer.

### T2. Scene openings: one plain anchor line
**Clannad:** A scene starts with a very short line that fixes time, place or routine, often with
no description: a two- or three-word period marker (lunch break, second period, nightfall), or
a repeat of yesterday's first line. Day two opens on the hill with the same
situation as day one (0415, 0416: "She's there again."). Day one opens with Tomoya's mood in
one flat sentence, and the scenery comes after.
**How we use it:** Open with one short line: a time header, a mood, or the same line as the last
time we were here. Scenery comes second. When a place comes back (the flat, room four, the
cafeteria), open with a line that mirrors the first visit.

### T3. Scene closings: a small image or a small wish, then out
**Clannad:** Days end on something small: a one-line wish, the walk home, falling asleep, or an
object noticed while leaving (0414 ends on the cassette player, 6800 ends on a warm morning and a
wish that it would last). The next scene often opens on the reverse of that line (sleep, then
someone waking him).
**How we use it:** End on a thing, a sound or a wish of one line or less. Never a summary. When
Bin falls asleep, the next scene can open on the reverse (waking, a voice).

### T4. Hesitation rhythm: trailing off, not being cut off
**Clannad:** In the common route plus After Story, about 3% of spoken lines are only an ellipsis
and about 3% start with "Um / Uh / Er / Well". Lines trail off with "..." all the time. Hard cut-offs
with a dash are rare (7 in about 12,800 spoken lines). People stop talking because they
lose their nerve, not because someone talks over them. The pattern is: a hesitant "Um...", then
"Well...", then a stalled search for words, and the listener gives up and fills the gap (0415,
first lines on the hill).
**Ours:** in 1,467 lines, 31 are only dots, 4 start with Um/Uh/Er/Well, and 15 are dash cut-offs.
**How we use it:** Use trailing-off hesitation in shy or scared moments (Lily hiding pain, the
ward kid, Luna, Ling Ling at the gate). Use dash cut-offs only for real interruptions. Meilin
never hesitates, which is her voice. So the one time she does, it means something. Hold that for
a later arc.

### T5. Running gag with a fixed punchline
**Clannad:** The same reply comes back word for word while the setups change. In 0415 Sunohara
lists every awful line of the rap, and Tomoya answers each one with the same flat apology
(fragment: "all I could come up with... Sorry."). Sanae's bread gag always has the same shape
(6800, twice): Akio insults the bread, someone says Sanae is behind him, she repeats the insult as
a wounded question ("So the bread I make is..."), she runs off, and Akio chases her shouting.
In 0424 every version of Sunohara's doorway skit ends with the same Kyou slap and the same
three lines.
**How we use it:** Once a gag has a shape, keep its last line identical and change only the
setup. The third time, change the ending.

### T6. Plant early, pay off later
**Clannad:** On day one (0414) Tomoya records a nonsense rap over Sunohara's tape. On day two
(0415) the victim plays it back at breakfast, and the reader hears the whole thing again from his
side. The reader enjoys it twice.
**How we use it:** When a gag or threat is set up (Old Qian's "charging HIM double", Jake's
sticky note, "pen awareness"), bring it back at least once, from a different angle or in
another character's mouth.

### T7. Slapstick formula (the Sunohara engine)
**Clannad:** (2416, 2418, 0424, 0429)
1. The straight man sets it up in a whispered side conversation in parentheses (Tomoya feeding
   Sunohara a pose or a "killer" line).
2. The victim is too confident and says so out loud.
3. The hit gets its own sound-effect line.
4. The final image is absurd and physical: only his shoe tips sticking out of a garbage chute, a
   slide down the corridor into a wall.
5. He bounces straight back with the same catchphrase, a whiny near-death complaint used twice
   in one scene.
6. Someone off to the side gives a deadpan verdict.
Repeated failures are told the same way each time. At the baseball game (0429) he pops up two
bunts, each narrated with nearly the same two lines.
**How we use it:** Bin is his own Sunohara. Every disaster ends with the same bounce-back move,
his star rating or his "Well.". Put a shouted or sound line inside every 3 lines of physical
comedy.

### T8. A misunderstanding that grows when you try to fix it
**Clannad:** At the family dinner (6800), a naive remark from Nagisa makes her father believe
Tomoya can't perform. The player chooses to clear it up. The explanation makes the father decide
the problem must be Nagisa's instead, and he pushes adult magazines on Tomoya while the mother
listens. Each fix makes it worse, and it ends with someone else caught out.
**How we use it:** When someone misreads Bin, let his correction make it worse. Innuendo only
between adults (style guide hard line). With Lily, keep the misreading innocent: bosses,
girlfriends, "workplace scandal".

### T9. One quiet line after the dumb banter
**Clannad:** The bread explodes, the parents run out, and the room goes calm. Then the girlfriend
says one small, sincere thing to Tomoya about his work (6800). The kindness lands because the
noise just stopped. In 0414, Tomoya leaves Sunohara's room and does something petty alone,
then admits to himself that they may not even be close friends.
**How we use it:** Every scene gets exactly one quiet line, placed right after the loudest joke.
Nobody comments on it, and the scene moves on.

### T10. Thoughts that argue with themselves
**Clannad:** Parenthesised thoughts start a sum or a plan and fail. On the walk to school
(0414) he tries to work out the minutes saved per year, then gives up. During the baseball game
(0429) the narrator catches himself doing play-by-play and asks why. Narration makes a decision
("I'll just stay quiet") and the next line does the opposite. His thoughts also flat-out
contradict the narration.
**How we use it:** Bin's parenthesised thoughts should correct or undercut the line before them.
p0Saturdays' air-conditioning pair is the model.

### T11. Choices: a half-finished thought, short options
**Clannad:** The prompt is narration that stops mid-thought (in 0416 he drifts off in class
wondering what to think about; in 6800 he tries to recall what day next Sunday is), and the
options finish the sentence in 1-4 words. Joke options exist.
Pick one and the story corrects you, then offers the menu again with one option changed
(6800: a festival, a bull run, a silly apron day; the bull run later becomes a run of Nagisa's
favourite snack). Branches are short and join back quickly.
**How we use it (future chapters only; don't edit existing choices):** The prompt is Bin's line or
thought left hanging. Options are a few words each, and a joke option can bounce back to the menu.
p5/EOS_AFTER's crystal sell-and-return loop already does this.

### T12. Emotional climaxes: short lines, unfinished sentences, one sense
**Clannad:** In the Fuko ending (1517) lines get very short (about 7-8 words), and one person
says one thing per line. Tomoya can't finish his big line ("I really love you. So...") and the
narration says that putting it into words would wash it away (end of 6800). The Kotomi flashback
(4512) is past tense, one action per line, with a single sense detail carrying the loss (smoke,
the pipe smell, a white apron). When the father leaves the bakery for a week (7200), all he gets
is two silence lines and a "Yeah...". Nobody gives a speech or explains how they feel.
**How we use it:** At the peak: short lines, a silence line, one concrete sense detail, and a
sentence that doesn't get finished. If a character explains the feeling, cut that line.

### T13. The joke comes back as the tear line
**Clannad:** Fuko's carved starfish runs as a joke across about 25 files (it's gross, it's cute,
nobody agrees). The ending (1518) replays her first meeting almost word for word: the same
gift, the same argument ("It's gross!" / "Starfish are cute!"). Now it's the emotional payoff.
**How we use it:** Our biggest emotional beats should reuse a line or object that was first a
joke: the milk-tea sleeve, "That's the whole thing", "Bin-Bin. / Yeah?".

### T14. A motif line that comes back reversed years later
**Clannad:** On day one at the foot of the hill, a girl says out loud that nothing stays the same
(fragment: "Nothing can stay the same forever."). Two years later (end of 6800) Tomoya thinks the
same words and realises he now wants the opposite of what he wanted on the hill. The hill itself
comes back across the opening week.
**How we use it:** Give Bin a first-scene line that the prologue ending turns around.
Ours: "pine smoke and iron" (a dream at 14) becomes the smell of a real place at 20.

### T15. One habit per side character; breaking it signals weight
**Clannad:** Sanae runs off in tears over her bread. Akio always shouts his love as he chases her.
Kotomi introduces herself with the same formula every time. Fuko refers to herself in the third
person and hands out starfish. Kyou slaps. Sunohara squeals. When a joker goes quiet (Akio's long
silence before he leaves in 7200), the reader knows it's serious.
**How we use it:** Leo films and eats. Meilin counts lies. Lily does "Bin-Bin." / "Yeah?". Jess
uses `[CJS]` and brackets. Peri eats popcorn and holds her starlight phone. For a serious beat,
have one of them drop the habit without saying so.

### T16. Day structure as rhythm
**Clannad:** Morning (the hill, one meeting) → class (staring out the window, time skipped in 1-3
lines) → lunch (group comedy) → after school (one quiet conversation) → night (Sunohara's room
or alone, a reflection). Transitions are bare lines, and boring time is skipped in a sentence.
**How we use it:** p1Daily already follows this. Keep a time header plus one mood line at each
jump. Skip dead time in one line instead of summarising it.

### T17. The narrator holds back what he knows
**Clannad:** Speaker tags change with the relationship: an unnamed voice, then "Girl", then her
surname, then her first name. Dream interludes (6900) show a snowy dead world, a girl's hand, an
empty room, and nothing explains them. Backstory (the father) is held back until a quiet evening.
The Fuko and Kotomi routes run on the narrator himself forgetting, so the reader learns only when
he remembers (1517: a teacher's offhand answer brings back a name).
**How we use it:** Bin's Haven past leaks only as smells, sounds and dream fragments, never
explained. The narrator can leave out his own weak moments and let someone else reveal them
later.

### T18. A chorus to widen the world
**Clannad:** In the Fuko ending (1518), two unnamed boys trade rumours over several days, and
through them the whole school starts waiting for a girl nobody has met.
**How we use it:** Chad's chat, the ward kids and campus passers-by work as our chorus. Use
them to show a reputation spreading, not to explain it.

**Global pass (T1, style guide "plain words").** v5 has about 65 intensifiers (completely,
entirely, terrifying, exceptionally, utterly...) in 1,467 lines. The sampled Clannad scripts have
about 33 in 8,100 lines, roughly a tenth of our density. In narrator lines (not Bin's mock-formal
jokes, where inflation is the joke), cut most of them. Target: narrator lines averaging 10-12 words
in comedy and 12 words or fewer at every climax (p0Bracelet gate, p3 rant, p8 portal).
